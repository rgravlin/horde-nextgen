# Module map

The module table, split out of [ARCHITECTURE.md](../../ARCHITECTURE.md). The index keeps the rules, the [data flow](data-flow.md) the runtimes and the [tests](tests.md) where each check lives.

## Module map and allowed imports

| Module | Owns | May import |
|---|---|---|
| `src/content/` | typed data only: `weapons.ts`, `enemies.ts` + `enemiesA.ts` (the archetype table, split by hand rather than by kind: `ENEMIES` spreads `ENEMIES_CORE` and `ENEMIES_A` and is the only name anyone imports, so a new chapter of bodies arrives in a new file), `powerups.ts`, and `levels/` (the chapter table, split the same way: `chapters-1-3.ts` + `chapters-4-6.ts` + `chapters-7-10.ts` in play order, joined and clamped by `index.ts`, which is also where a chapter's elite roster gets resolved against `elites.ts`; the `LevelDef` schema is in `types.ts` and the floor, script and tint shorthands every chapter file is written with are in `builders.ts`). A table's schema lives beside it (`weapons.ts`, `enemies.ts`, `powerups.ts`, `levels/types.ts`), never in a shared types file | nothing but the `sim` ids it shares (`PropKind`/`ZoneKind`, `EdgeId`) |
| `src/sim/` | `world.ts` (`World` and `createWorld`, plus the re-export of the three modules cut out of it so the sim, the render layer and the tests still come in through one door: `pools.ts` — the struct-of-arrays pool and its `alloc`/`release`/`countAlive`; `constants.ts` — the pool caps, the tuning in one `TUNE` and the `Ev`/`Tele` event ids, each written down once; `events.ts` — the event ring and the blast stack), `step.ts` (system order), `input.ts` (the `InputFrame` type), `rng.ts` (xoshiro128**), `spatialHash.ts`, `hash.ts`, `systems/{weapons,status,enemyProjectiles,pickups,powerups,waves,boss,drone}.ts` (besides its pass, `drone.ts` exports `droneOrbit(tick, playerX, playerY, out)` — the one answer to "where is the companion", which its own muzzle is loosed from and a drawn drone is meant to be drawn at, so the two cannot be drawn apart), `systems/projectiles/` (the shot physics: `shared.ts` holds the one way a hit lands and the padding every query uses, `ray.ts` the hitscan, beam and chain marches, `melee.ts` the arc sweep, `flyers.ts` everything a round does while it is in the air — flight, `steer`, `drag` and `burst`), `systems/enemies/` (the enemy pass: `shared.ts` holds the drive and the tick's travel scratch plus the one findability fact the whole tick reads — `underground(phase)` and the `markFindable` pass that refills `hash.findable` before `step.ts` rebuilds the grid, so a body under the floor is in no query at all rather than excepted in thirteen places, then one module per movement family — `seekers.ts`, `rushers.ts`, `standoff.ts`, `support.ts`, `marksmen.ts` (the sniper and the orbiter, which share one bargain: a line is painted, held, then spent along it), `burrowers.ts` (the same bargain crossed under the floor: untargetable while it travels, a ring for its burst, both halves pinned in `tests/unit/enemies-e.test.ts`), `phaser.ts` (the one body that does not travel through the space between two points: the line it paints is exactly as long as the line it spends), `nursery.ts` (the spawner: no shot and no burst, its damage is other bodies, and it keeps `keepMin` metres from the player while it still has a brood to pay), `bosses.ts` — plus `spawn.ts`, `update.ts`, `behaviours.ts`, `contact.ts` and `deaths.ts`), `systems/obstacles.ts` (the one cover + floor-hazard path: swept circle-vs-box and circle-vs-circle push-out, and the hazard clocks), `systems/levels.ts` (the chapter arc: which floor is under the player, and what its script ends on), `serialize/` (the save: whole pools and the floor as bytes, scalars as numbers — `index.ts` is the format and the two entry points, `blobs.ts` the byte codec, `pools.ts` the column tables, `record.ts` the shape checks a restore runs), `replay.ts` (records a run's input and plays it back). The pool caps and one `TUNE` of tuning constants are in `constants.ts`; there is no separate `config.ts` | `src/content` only |
| `src/render/` | `renderer.ts` (the assembly: the tier's numbers put onto the renderer, the per-World kit rebuilt when the World changes, one guard around the draw, and the `Backend`/`Quality` re-export the callers come in through), `machine.ts` (booting the `WebGPURenderer` and letting `renderer.init()` resolve the backend, then the four facts `pickTier` reads: adapter name, `deviceMemory`, `userAgentData`, user agent), `stage.ts` (the room: fog, sky, the HDRI as reflections only, the floor, the one key light and the shadow box that travels with the player, and the chapter tint), `kit.ts` (one World's layers — cover, hazards, batches, crates, telegraphs, particles — built and disposed as one set, in that order), `cameraRig.ts` (the 40°/55°/22 m rig: the damped follow with its aim lead, the title orbit, the shake on top of `lookAt`, and the pointer's ray onto the floor), `feel.ts` (this frame's `Ev.Shake`/`Ev.Explosion` into trauma and chromatic punch, read through the layer's own cursor), `playerRig.ts` (the player's body, its trim and the three gun shapes, turned by whatever yaw it is handed), `facing.ts` (the one answer to "which way is the player's gun pointing": it reads the tick's muzzle line off the World rather than re-deriving it, and turns that line into the rig's yaw, because the kit's guns point along local +z and the floor's north is render -z, and because under auto-fire the line is a body rather than the cursor; a companion's *position* has its answer on the sim side, in `systems/drone.ts`'s `droneOrbit`, and the render layer takes that call rather than writing a second orbit that can drift from the rounds), `instancing.ts` (one instanced pair per archetype — a body batch and an accent-core batch — over a look it may share with other archetypes, plus rounds, shards and the dash trail), `chests.ts` (the supply crate's two layers: an amber crate and the additive pillar over it, one instanced batch each cut to the crate pool, both shrunk by the crate's remaining `life` so it spends its last six seconds telling you it is going), `meshes/` (the procedural geometry and materials those batches draw: `primitives.ts` — the part-merge toolkit and the triangle-count readout, `shapes.ts` — the archetype recipes keyed by mesh id and their cache, `shapesC.ts` — the three recipes keyed by archetype instead (teleporter, burrower, spawner), spread into `SHAPES` so they come through `hordeMesh` like the rest, `props.ts` — the projectile, supply-crate-and-beacon, player-kit and telegraph shapes, `materials.ts` — the three materials and the two per-instance flash/dissolve attributes; the folder comes in through its `index.ts`, so the callers still import `./meshes`), `scenery.ts` (the floor's hazard discs), `telegraphs.ts` (the Threat Red charge strips, landing rings and sniper sights), `particles.ts` (the live spark path: four palette batches, TSL compute on a capable backend and the same maths on a CPU-filling loop otherwise, sized from the tier's particle budget), `fxView.ts` (an earlier single-field CPU particle view kept beside it; nothing imports `createFxView` any more), `lights.ts` (the pooled point lights), `assets.ts` (the concrete floor, the Kenney kit cover and the HDRI environment). The quality tier is read by `assets.ts`, `lights.ts`, `instancing.ts` and `renderer.ts`, which is where the Low/Med/High knobs live. | `three`, `three/webgpu`, `three/tsl`, read-only `sim`/`content` |
| `src/audio/` | `synth.ts` (WebAudio graph), `sequencer.ts` (the music clock), `songs.ts`, `sfx.ts` (event id → voice) | read-only `sim` events; no engine import |
| `src/ui/` | `hud/` (the corner readouts, the boss plate and the edge markers: `index.ts` builds the layout and reads the World once per frame, `paint.ts` is the write-nothing-twice helpers, `markers.ts` the edge arrows and the pool sweep that aims them, `hands.ts` what a weapon's gauge says), `radar.ts` (the top-left map, painted on one 2D canvas), `levelup.ts` (the three-card offer), `menus/` (the screen panels: `index.ts` the panels themselves, `rows.ts` the Settings and Controls tables, `listen.ts` the capture-phase key listener the rebind rows borrow), `styles.css` (DESIGN.md palette/type scale for the whole overlay) | read-only `sim`/`game` state; no engine import |
| `src/input/` | `sampler.ts` (keyboard + mouse + pad → `InputFrame`), `gamepad.ts` (the polled pad: two dead-zoned sticks, edge-tracked buttons, one of two face-button tables per frame), `bindings.ts` (default map, remappable — pads are not in it) | `sim` types and `sim` constants only |
| `src/game/` | `app.ts` (scene state machine: which scene a frame leaves, when the sim is not run at all, and the `save`/`load` commands — it decides *when* a run is written and delegates the timing), `appTypes.ts` (the shapes the shell shares with `main.ts` and the browser scenarios — `SceneId`, `ForgeState`, `ForgeDebug`, `App`, `AppPorts` — plus `snapshotSettings`, so a dial read back out of `__forgeState().settings` comes through the same door the shell writes it through), `appState.ts` (`buildState` and its `nearestEnemies`/`measureFps` helpers, all driven through a `StateHost` argument so a test can paint a state out of a bare `World` with no app), `appDebug.ts` (`__forgeDebug` over a `DebugHost`: setSeed, spawn, grantXp, giveWeapon, bench, arena; it reads the live world because a bench window arms a new run), `appArchive.ts` (`keepShelf` and its `Shelf` host: the autosave clock, the meta rows, the shelf flag Continue and Load read; the four *when* rules of a save, which is the part no test of the storage layer can see), `loop.ts` (fixed-step accumulator), `persistence/` (the three storage slots: `envelope.ts` the `StorageLike` port and the JSON read/write, `keys.ts` the three names, `runArchive.ts` the run slot, `settings.ts` and `meta.ts` the other two payloads and their checks, `autosave.ts` the autosave clock; `index.ts` re-exports the whole surface), `bench.ts` (the browser bench rig and its report) | `sim`, `content`, `input`, and the render/audio/ui modules |
| `src/main.ts` | composition root: build the `World`, the renderer, audio, UI and input, register `window.__forgeState` / `window.__forgeDebug` | everything |

Hard rules, checked in CI:

- `tests/unit/purity.test.ts` greps `src/sim/**` and `src/content/**` for `three`, `document`, `window`,
  `localStorage`, `performance.now` and timer calls and fails on any hit.
- `render`, `audio` and `ui` never import each other and never write to the `World`; they only consume it. Only
  `src/sim/` mutates game state; `src/game/` and `src/input/` feed it.
- `src/audio` and `src/ui` must not import from `src/render` — a headless run with rendering disabled still plays
  sound and updates the HUD.
- Anything touching `window` or `localStorage` goes through a port (`StorageLike`, `Clock`) injected from
  `main.ts`/`game`, so unit tests pass in-memory stubs and Node stays green.
